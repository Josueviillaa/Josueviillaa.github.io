## What it's about

Dutybound sets up the storefront of an **airport duty-free shop**: a shopping
cart and a checkout flow that, before you pay, "scans" a boarding pass to decide
your tax situation. Almost all of the interesting surface revolves around **how
the backend interprets that boarding pass** and what it does afterwards with
what it extracts from it.

## The path, in concepts

Without going into the how, the route touched four ideas I found very
instructive:

- **Reversing a proprietary format blind.** The server expects a strict,
  structured data format. There's no documentation for the parser: I rebuilt it
  **using its own error messages as a compass**, satisfying it field by field
  until I mapped the whole structure.
- **Misplaced trust in client data.** A "business" decision the server treated as
  trustworthy turned out to derive from data the client controls 100%. A classic
  *broken access control* once you think about it, but well hidden behind layers
  of parsing.
- **Jumping from one layer to another.** A flaw in one layer of the application
  ended up giving execution in a completely different layer of the stack. The
  lesson: follow the data to where it crosses a **trust boundary**.
- **Lateral movement and escalation by design, not by CVE.** Neither foothold nor
  root came from a public exploit: they were **misconfigurations and design
  mistakes** (trust, permissions, session exposure). Enumerating beat assuming,
  every time.

## Takeaways

- **Don't marry the narrative that "fits".** I chased a vector that made perfect
  thematic sense... and it was a dead end. Stopping to **verify and discard it**
  before investing more time was what unblocked the box. The nice hypothesis is
  not evidence.
- **Error messages are free documentation.** A verbose parser draws its own
  structure for you if you listen.
- **"Trusted data" almost never is.** If the user can influence a value, assume
  they will, with bad intent.
- **Methodology matters more than the trick.** Enumerate thoroughly, map privilege
  boundaries and follow the data: that's what solved it, not a magic one-liner.

## A professional pentest mindset

While solving it I kept contrasting the "quick CTF" route with how I'd do it in a
real engagement:

- Confirm findings **non-destructively** before going for execution.
- **Minimise impact**: prefer reading/extracting over modifying, and back up any
  file that does have to be touched.
- **Document every boundary crossed** (what data, from where, to where) as an
  independent finding, even if they form a single chain.
- **Cleanup**: leave no artifacts (binaries, modified files) after the PoC.
